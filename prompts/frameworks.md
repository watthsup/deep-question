# KNOWLEDGE · FRAMEWORKS

Reference material for the Behavioral Question Designer. Use what fits; name what you use.

## 1. SPIN Selling (Rackham) — the conversation arc

| Stage | Purpose | Good question shape | Bad question shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | Learn context without friction | "อะไรทำให้คุณเริ่มสนใจเรื่องนี้ตอนนี้" | "คุณมีประกันกี่กรมธรรม์" (feels like an audit) |
| **Problem** | Surface dissatisfaction or worry the person already half-knows | "ตอนนี้อะไรที่คุณกังวลที่สุดถ้าต้องเข้าโรงพยาบาลจริง" | "คุณมีปัญหาอะไร" (too open, too heavy) |
| **Implication** | Let the person feel the cost of leaving the problem alone | "ถ้าค่ารักษาเกินสวัสดิการ ส่วนต่างจะมาจากไหน" | "คุณรู้ไหมว่า…" (lecturing) |
| **Need-Payoff** | The person states the value themselves | "ถ้ามีแผนนี้แล้ว อะไรจะทำให้คุณสบายใจที่สุด" | "คุณอยากได้ผลประโยชน์อะไร" (product-centric) |

Rule of thumb: Implication questions are the most powerful and the most dangerous. One per conversation, softened by a micro-reflection that immediately offers a way out.

## 2. GAP analysis — assumption vs reality

Structure: **What they think they have → what it actually covers → what a real event costs → the gap.** Use this to design the `gap` step and its micro-reflections. Typical Thai gaps:

- Company welfare (ประกันกลุ่ม) covers routine IPD but caps room and surgery; ends when employment ends.
- Social security (ประกันสังคม) covers treatment at the registered hospital only; no private-hospital choice.
- Universal coverage (บัตรทอง) covers treatment but not income loss, not private room, not targeted therapies outside the list.
- Savings: people anchor on "I have some savings" without pricing a multi-year illness.

Never state a gap as an accusation. State it as a discovery the person makes ("หลายคนเพิ่งรู้ตอนเบิกว่า…").

## 3. Maslow — which need the product really serves

| Level | Insurance meaning | Products that live here |
|---|---|---|
| Physiological / **Safety** | "I will be treated well and my savings survive" | Health lumpsum, deductible health, cancer, critical illness, PA |
| **Love / Belonging** | "I will not be a burden; I take care of the people I love" | Senior health for parents, senior life (legacy, funeral), health for children/spouse |
| **Esteem** | "I make smart, responsible decisions" | Savings-life (Gen Life), deductible plans chosen for efficiency, Google-intent buyers |
| Self-actualisation | "I live the life I want without fear" | Lifestyle framing on Instagram; PA for active people |

Pick the level the person is actually in, not the one the product brochure claims.

## 4. FAB — Feature → Advantage → Benefit

Translate every product mechanism into a personal benefit before it appears in copy.

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit (what to say) |
|---|---|---|
| Deductible 30,000 | Lower premium | "จ่ายเบี้ยถูกลงมาก แล้วยังกันเหตุใหญ่ที่ทำเงินเก็บหาย" |
| Lump-sum cancer payout | Cash on diagnosis | "ได้เงินก้อนทันทีที่ตรวจพบ จะรักษาที่ไหน หยุดงานนานแค่ไหน ก็ตัดสินใจเองได้" |
| Guaranteed issue senior life | No health questions | "ไม่ต้องตอบคำถามสุขภาพ อายุเท่านี้ก็ยังทำได้" |
| Cash-back savings life | Liquidity during term | "มีเงินคืนระหว่างทาง ไม่ต้องรอครบสัญญา" |

## 5. Feel–Felt–Found — the shape of a micro-reflection

"I understand you feel X. Many people in your situation felt the same. What they found was Y." Compress to two sentences in Thai. Example for "I already have company welfare":

> เข้าใจเลย มีสวัสดิการอยู่แล้วก็อุ่นใจระดับหนึ่ง หลายคนเพิ่งมาเห็นตอนต้องนอนโรงพยาบาลว่าค่าห้องกับค่าผ่าตัดเกินวงเงิน ส่วนต่างตรงนั้นแหละที่ควรมีแผนรอง

## 6. Cognitive biases — one per step, used honestly

| Bias | Mechanism | Ethical use in this context | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loss aversion** | Losses hurt ~2× more than equivalent gains | Frame protection of existing savings, not acquisition of benefits | Catastrophising, graphic illness imagery |
| **Social proof** | People follow similar others | "คนวัยเดียวกันจำนวนมากเริ่มมองเรื่องนี้ตอนอายุประมาณนี้" (qualitative) | Fabricated counts, fake reviews |
| **Anchoring** | First number frames later numbers | Show a realistic treatment cost, then premium per day | Inflated cost anchors |
| **Availability** | Recent, vivid events feel more likely | Ask about proximity ("มีคนใกล้ตัวป่วยไหม") so the person supplies the vivid example themselves | Supplying fear imagery yourself |
| **Commitment & consistency** | Small yeses lead to larger yeses | Ask easy questions first; reflect back their own words in later steps | Trick questions |
| **Scarcity** | Value rises with perceived limited availability | Only real facts: premium rises with age; guaranteed-issue has age caps | Fake countdowns, fake limited slots |
| **Hyperbolic discounting** | Now is overweighted vs later | Instant reflection after every answer; "เริ่มความคุ้มครองได้เลยวันนี้" if true | Pressure to buy now |
| **Framing** | Same fact, different frame, different choice | "จ่ายวันละ 40 บาท" vs "ปีละ 14,600" — both true, choose the humane one | Hiding the annual figure entirely |
| **Endowment** | We value what feels already ours | Micro-reflections that speak as if the protection is already theirs ("แผนของคุณ") | Implying a contract exists |
| **Default / status quo** | People keep the default | Pre-order options so the recommended one is easy to see | Pre-selecting answers |

## 7. The five persuasion archetypes (Dark-AI marketing lens, ethical version)

| Archetype | Primary biases | Tone | Signals that a segment is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fear-driven** | Loss aversion, availability | Calm, protective, "let's make sure" | Facebook family buyers, recent proximity to illness, insured in the upper half of 23–45 or in 46–60, parents buying for a small child (0–22) |
| **Instant gratification** | Hyperbolic discounting, framing | Short, numeric, immediate payoff | TikTok, insured 23–45 applying for themselves (younger half) |
| **Smart decision maker** | Anchoring, framing | Clear, comparative, respects intelligence | Google intent, deductible products, men 23–60, savings-life buyers, parents planning education for a school-age child (0–22) |
| **Social-driven** | Social proof, belonging | Warm, "people like you", lifestyle | Instagram, women 23–45 |
| **Risk-averse** | Commitment & consistency, permission | Gentle, unhurried, reassuring, zero pressure | Insured 60+ and the retiring end of 46–60, senior products, adult children buying for a parent, first-time buyers of any age |

Pick one primary archetype; you may name a secondary. Everything else (sentence length, which bias per step, how strong the implication question is) follows from this choice.

## 8. Micro-reflection principles

1. Validate first, always. The person just told you something personal.
2. Add exactly one new idea (a reframe, a fact, a permission).
3. Bridge forward ("ขั้นต่อไปเราจะดูว่า…") so the next question feels earned.
4. Never repeat the option text back verbatim; paraphrase to show understanding.
5. Length: 15–35 Thai words. TikTok and insured 23–45 applying for themselves (younger half): 10–20. Insured 60+: stay short too, but slower and warmer, never clipped.
6. For health-condition answers: no judgement, no "unfortunately". State what is still possible.

## 9. What the TSR needs from the answers

Every step should let the TSR infer at least one of: **trigger** (why now), **pain** (what they fear), **readiness** (budget, existing cover), **eligibility** (health, age, occupation), **emotional driver** (what relief they want). Write `underlying_intent` so a TSR skimming the brief in 20 seconds gets the emotional picture without reading the Thai copy.
